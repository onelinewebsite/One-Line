Printing methods & finishes

Learn how different print methods work, how they look and feel, and where they are commonly used.

PRINTING METHOD

DTF Print

How it is made

Direct-to-film printing creates the artwork on a special PET film. Adhesive powder is cured onto the inks; a heat press bonds the printed layer to the garment.

Look and feel

Sharp multicolour graphics and solid logos, usually with a smooth surface layer and some texture you can feel.

Common uses

Detailed multicolour logos, names, and varied garment quantities.

Material and finish notes

It is a surface transfer, so the hand-feel depends on artwork coverage, adhesive, fabric and pressing conditions. Stretch and wash resistance vary.

PRINTING METHOD

Embroidery

How it is made

Thread is stitched directly through the garment with a programmed embroidery machine and backing.

Look and feel

Raised stitches, visible thread texture and a premium tactile finish. The design becomes part of the garment, rather than a printed ink layer.

Common uses

Company marks, badges, initials, polos and small logos.

Material and finish notes

Tiny writing, thin lines, gradients and photographic detail can be difficult to reproduce in stitches.

PRINTING METHOD

Sublimation

How it is made

Heat turns special dye into gas so it bonds with compatible polyester fibres rather than leaving a thick layer on top.

Look and feel

Colours integrate into the fabric; there is usually little or no raised surface where the design is dyed.

Common uses

Full-coverage sportswear, polyester jerseys, bright patterns and continuous artwork.

Material and finish notes

Best suited to polyester and suitable light bases; results on dark cotton garments are not comparable to polyester.

PRINTING METHOD

Screen Print

How it is made

A stencil on a mesh screen lets ink pass onto selected areas of fabric, generally with separate screens for each colour.

Look and feel

Solid shapes and opaque colour; the feel can be thin or textured depending on the ink and deposit.

Common uses

Repeated logos, one- or few-colour designs and larger production quantities.

Material and finish notes

Fine colour gradients and very small detail can require more complex setups; ink type changes surface feel.

PRINTING METHOD

Other printing methods

How it is made

Printing methods outside the listed categories may use transfers, direct inks, heat presses or specialised embellishments.

Look and feel

Texture, colour coverage and durability depend on the exact process and garment.

Common uses

Special finishes and custom methods depending on equipment.

Material and finish notes

The appearance cannot be inferred from the method name alone.

Results vary with garment material, equipment, artwork and production conditions.
